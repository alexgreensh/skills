## Session flow

### Part 1 — Camera

Ask: "What camera are you planning to use?"

Walk them through the options based on their answer:

**Phone:**
Completely viable in today's world. Works for studio setups and vlogging. Minimal setup. If this is what they have, it's enough to start.

Descript integration note: With Descript, you can upload video files from your phone directly into your project — so the phone-to-edit pipeline is seamless.

**Mirrorless camera (Sony, Canon, Nikon, Fuji, etc.):**
Two parts: camera body + lens. For lenses:

- **Prime lenses** — fixed focal length (e.g. 35mm). Sharper and often faster (wider aperture), but no zoom.
- **Zoom lenses** — range of focal lengths (e.g. 24–70mm). More versatile; Ramdy calls the 24–70 the "best friend" lens because it covers almost every use case.

How focal length affects your shot:
- **Lower focal length (wider)** = more in frame, good for run-and-gun/vlogs (e.g. 16mm)
- **Higher focal length (telephoto)** = more compressed look, good for stationary setups or distance shooting (e.g. 70–200mm)

If they don't have a camera yet: "Do your own research based on your budget and use case. There are great options at every price point. Don't buy gear just because Ramdy has it."

### Part 2 — Exposure triangle

If they're shooting on a camera (not just a phone), make sure they understand the three settings that control exposure. They don't need to master them now — just understand what each one does.

**1. Shutter speed — set this first**
Controls how long the sensor is exposed to light. Also controls motion blur.
- Too fast = choppy, no motion blur
- Too slow = blurry, more light but less sharp
- **Golden rule:** Set shutter speed to 2× your frame rate. (Shooting 24fps? Set to 1/50. Shooting 60fps? Set to 1/120.) Set it once and leave it unless you're going for a stylized look.

**2. Aperture (f-stop) — set this second**
The opening in the lens. Controls light and depth of field.
- Low f-number (e.g. f/1.8) = more light, blurry background (bokeh)
- High f-number (e.g. f/11) = less light, more background in focus
- Set it based on the depth of field you want. If the environment is too bright at a low aperture, use an ND filter (sunglasses for your lens).

**3. ISO — touch this last**
Artificially brightens the image, but introduces grain/noise. ISO is a last resort.
- Each camera has a native ISO — the cleanest baseline. (Sony A7IV: 800 and 3200.)
- Stay at your native ISO whenever possible. Adjust lighting or aperture first.
- Ramdy's analogy: relying on ISO to fix your exposure is like relying on salt to fix a bad recipe. More doesn't equal better.

If any of this sounds like a foreign language, that's fine. Understanding what each setting does is more important than mastering them right now.

### Part 3 — Tripods

Short on this one:

- Most tripods work fine as long as they fit your camera mount.
- Don't skimp. People spend thousands on cameras and then put them on a wobbly $20 tripod. Get something reputable that can hold the weight of your camera.
- You don't need to spend a lot. Just don't buy garbage.

### Part 4 — Audio

Ramdy's rule: **Good audio does 90% of the work.** It is heavily underrated. Don't over-focus on how cinematic your video looks and ignore your audio.

Three options:

**Lavalier mic ($20–$40)**
- Clips to your shirt. Omnidirectional (picks up from all around it — no pointing required).
- Plug into camera, phone, or an audio receiver.
- Best starter option if they want better audio without thinking too much about it.

**Cardioid mic (USB or XLR)**
- Better for stationary setups (desk, podcasting).
- Unidirectional — great at capturing vocals, rejects background noise. But needs to be close to your mouth, so it'll be in the shot.
- Needs a stand/arm and possibly an audio interface.
- More setup, but great sound for a sit-down style.

**Shotgun mic**
- Picks up audio from a distance, so no mic in the shot.
- Requires more rigging (boom pole or C-stand + audio interface) — can be complex.
- Best for filmmaking-style setups where no mic in frame is required.

Any of these is better than your camera's built-in mic.

Descript note: Once your footage is in Descript, you can run **Studio Sound** to clean up background noise and enhance the audio quality — especially useful if your mic wasn't perfect.

### Part 5 — Lighting

Ramdy's rule: **95% of what makes a shot look good is lighting.** Not the camera. Not the lens.

**Three-point lighting setup (the baseline):**
1. **Key light** — main light on your subject, positioned slightly off to the side
2. **Fill light** — fills in shadows from the key light; intensity depends on how much shadow you want
3. **Back/edge light** — behind the subject; adds separation from the background or lights the background itself

Even cheap lights from Amazon can replicate this setup effectively.

**The free option:** A window. Natural light during the day is more than enough to get started. Just face toward it (light source in front, not behind you). Don't film at night with only house lights.

Ask: "What's your lighting situation like — artificial lights, natural light, or nothing yet?"

Help them figure out a workable setup with what they have.

### Part 6 — Performance

If they're nervous about being on camera, address it directly:

> "Being in front of a camera is weird. You're talking to a black box pretending it's a human. That's not normal. But it becomes second nature with enough reps.
>
> Ramdy describes himself as one of the most shy, introverted people he knows. You probably wouldn't guess that from his videos."

There's no shortcut to feeling comfortable on camera. The only path is doing it.

**For anyone struggling to even start:** Don't try to film your first "real" video yet. Start smaller:
- Record yourself getting ready in the morning. Think out loud.
- Film yourself going on a walk, doing laundry, playing games.
- Don't edit these. Don't post them. Just get used to being recorded.

When that starts to feel normal, you're ready.

### Part 7 — Filming best practices

Four rules Ramdy follows every shoot:

**1. Test your shot first.**
Film 10–20 seconds of test footage with camera and mic before recording for real. Review it. Check your background, your clothes, your hair. Hyper-analyze the frame. "There's nothing worse than finishing a shoot and realizing you had something in your teeth the whole time." (This may or may not have happened to Ramdy.)

**2. Record multiple takes — always.**
Even if your first take was great. Even if you're sick of doing it. Always have at least two usable takes. More takes = more flexibility in editing. The extra take is insurance, not perfectionism.

**3. Audio sync clap (if recording audio separately).**
If camera and mic are recording on separate devices, clap visibly and audibly at the start of each recording session. This sync point makes matching audio to video in editing fast and easy.

**4. Dump your footage immediately after filming.**
As soon as you're done, transfer files to your computer and organize them. Don't leave raw footage sitting on a card or phone any longer than you have to.

### Descript API — Create project after filming

> The Descript API is early access and actively evolving. These skills describe **intent** — what to do and when. Your MCP connection handles **how** — exact parameters, error handling, and polling. If anything has changed, [docs.descriptapi.com](https://docs.descriptapi.com/) is the source of truth.

**When:** After footage is dumped onto the computer and files are organized. Before opening Descript manually.

**What to do:** Create a new Descript project for this video and import the footage.

**Operation:** `POST /jobs/import/project_media`

Intent to convey to your MCP tool:
- Create a new project named after this video (e.g., `[Channel] — Ep [#] — [Working Title]`)
- Import the video and audio files by providing their URLs
- Optionally set `team_access` if collaborators need access

**File URL requirement:** The API requires files to be accessible via URL with HTTP Range request support — local file paths don't work. The creator needs to upload files to a URL-accessible location first (Google Drive shared link, Dropbox, S3, etc.). Pre-signed URLs should be valid for 12–48 hours.

**After submitting:** Poll `GET /jobs/{job_id}` until `job_state` is `"stopped"`. The response includes a `project_url` — surface this to the creator so they can open the project directly.

**Current limitation:** Importing into an *existing* project via the API is marked as work in progress. For first-time project creation this endpoint is fully supported.

**If Descript MCP is not configured:** The creator opens Descript manually, creates a new project, and imports files through the Descript UI. This is the workflow covered in Episode 5.
