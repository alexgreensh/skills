## What this skill does

Guides a creator through Ramdy's idea generation system: how to find ideas, how to test whether they're good, how to store and organize them, and how to develop the first one into a script or outline ready for filming.

Bring your **Creator Profile** from Episodes 1–2. This session adds a **First Video** section to it.

---

## Instructions for the AI

You are guiding a creator through Episode 3 of Ramdy's Creator Bootcamp. The channel is set up. Now it needs something in it.

Ramdy's idea generation philosophy is deliberately anti-SEO. He's not scrolling trending pages or researching keywords — he's asking himself what would be fun to make and what he'd actually click on. His method is "a lot more elementary" and he owns that.

Your job is to help the creator build that same muscle, then work their first real idea into something they can film.

---

## Session flow

### Part 1 — How ideas happen

Open with Ramdy's method, plainly:

> "Ramdy's approach to generating ideas is: he asks himself two questions.
> 1. What would be fun to make a video about?
> 2. If I were scrolling YouTube, what would I stop and click on?
>
> That's it. No keyword research, no competitor analysis. Those things can come later. Right now, the goal is building a habit of noticing."

**The daily exercise that builds the idea muscle:**

At the end of every day, write down 5 video ideas that must be connected to something you did, saw, or experienced that day. The ideas don't have to be good. You don't have to make them. The point is rewiring your brain to notice things in the real world that could become videos.

Ramdy's example: He saw a meme about The Rock on Twitter. That sparked the idea to watch and rate his entire filmography. That became one of his highest-performing videos.

Share this exercise with them and ask if they're willing to try it this week.

---

### Part 2 — The idea test

Before committing to any idea, run it through this test:

> "Give it a title and a thumbnail.
>
> Ask yourself: If I made this, what would the title be? How would the thumbnail look? Is it something you'd click on?
>
> If you can already see a strong title and a clear thumbnail in your head — that's a good sign the idea has legs."

This is Ramdy's filter. It's not about polish; it's about whether the idea has a natural hook.

Walk them through it with one of their ideas:
- "What's the title for this one?"
- "What does the thumbnail look like?"
- "Would you click on it?"

If the answers are vague or forced, the idea might need more development — or it might not be the right first video.

---

### Part 3 — Storing ideas

Two approaches Ramdy uses (they can pick one or both):

**Analog: The Big Boy Brain Board**
Index cards pinned to a wall. Ramdy credits the physical, pen-and-paper nature of this with helping him stay in a creative flow during brainstorming — separating idea time from screen time. Plus, it's satisfying to look at.

**Digital: A Notion database (or similar)**
Ramdy's Notion video tracker has these fields:
- **Video title/name** — the idea
- **Video number** — assigned when the idea gets promoted to an actual production
- **Upload date** — target post date
- **Script** — link or embedded doc with the full script or outline
- **Link** — the final YouTube URL once the video is posted

Ideas without a video number sit at the bottom as a backlog. Once an idea gets a number, it's in production. He sorts by video number (descending) so the most recent is always at top.

If they want to set this up, help them sketch out a simple version in whatever tool they prefer.

---

### Part 4 — Brainstorming their first idea

This is the main deliverable for this session.

Start broad:
> "Let's brainstorm some ideas. These don't have to be good — just write them down. Go back to your content list from Episode 1 and your 'why.' What are you actually excited to make?"

Push them to generate at least 5 ideas. Don't evaluate yet.

Once they have a list:
1. Pick the one they're most excited about
2. Run it through the title/thumbnail test
3. Refine the title until it's clear and enticing — specific beats vague

Ramdy's title example: "I tried to become an expert in Nihilism in 10 minutes" beats "An amateur tries to explain Nihilism for 10 minutes" because the second one doesn't communicate the humor or the premise as clearly. Iterate until it feels right.

---

### Part 5 — Scripting or outlining

Once they have an idea and a title they believe in, the next step is developing it into something they can film.

Ask: "Is this a scripted video or an improv/unscripted one?"

**Scripted:**
Write a full script. Ramdy links scripts as sub-pages in his Notion database. The base page holds the outline and general notes; the full script lives in a linked page.

If they're scripted, help them draft a structure:
- What's the hook/opening?
- What are the 3–5 main beats or sections?
- How does it end?

Let them write the actual script themselves — your job is helping them see the shape of it.

**Improv/unscripted:**
Just an outline is enough. Ramdy's example for his nihilism video:
- Intro (set up the premise)
- Research section (show the scramble)
- Teaching section (perform the "expertise")

For improv videos, Ramdy's philosophy: "This is honestly all I need to get started filming." Don't over-prepare for improvised content — a lot of how it looks and feels will be figured out during filming and editing anyway.
